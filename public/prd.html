01Purpose

Orderbook Lab is a browser-based simulation of a limit order book. Synthetic orders arrive, join a queue, trade against each other and are cancelled; the resulting book, trades and price are drawn on screen as it happens.

Its real purpose is to be built in public. Every line of the application is written by an AI agent responding to a Jira ticket, and this document is the specification those tickets are written against. It exists to demonstrate that a well-written requirement is sufficient input for working, tested, deployed software.

This is a Level 3 book — and the distinction matters

Market data is described by the depth of detail it carries. The three levels are not degrees of accuracy but genuinely different pictures of the same market:

LevelWhat it showsPrice levels
Level 1Aggregated volumeBest price only
Level 2Aggregated volumeEvery price level
Level 3Individual orders, each with its place in queueEvery price level

Orderbook Lab models the book at Level 3. Every order is a distinct entity from the moment it arrives until it is filled or cancelled, and it holds a position in the queue at its price level. This is what makes price–time priority (INV-3) a meaningful property rather than a formality, and it is why a cancellation removes one order rather than a whole price level.

Where the interface aggregates for legibility — the depth ladder in REQ-7 shows total resting volume and a count of resting orders per level — that is a display choice over a Level 3 book underneath, not a Level 2 model. REQ-8 exposes the individual queue directly.

This is a demonstrator, not a product. It is not affiliated with, and must not be presented as, any BMLL product. Every value on screen is generated in the browser — the constraint is stated once, properly, as NFR-1.

03Scope

Version 1.0 is a complete but deliberately unembellished application. It must be genuinely working and genuinely correct — not a mock-up — while leaving obvious, visible room for enhancement. That headroom is the demonstration surface, and is listed in §8.

In scope

  • A Level 3 matching engine tracking individual orders and their queue position
  • Limit orders, market orders and cancellations
  • A seeded synthetic order flow generator
  • A depth ladder, a queue view at the touch, a trade tape, a price chart and a top-of-book readout
  • Playback controls

Not in scope

  • Any server, persistence, authentication, or network call at runtime
  • Order modification — see §8
  • Multiple venues, iceberg or hidden orders, auction phases, or self-trade prevention

04Functional requirements

Each requirement carries a stable identifier. Tickets cite them, and the acceptance criteria below become test names verbatim — see §9.

Throughout, queue position means an order's rank among the orders resting at the same price on the same side, ordered by arrival time. Position 1 is next to fill.

REQ-1

Limit orders rest individually and join the back of the queue

A limit order that cannot immediately execute is added to the book as a distinct order at its price level, taking the last queue position at that price. It retains its identity and its position until it is filled or cancelled. Several orders may rest at the same price.

Acceptance criteria

  • Given an empty book, when a buy limit order of 100 at 99 is submitted, then price level 99 holds one order of size 100 at queue position 1.
  • Given a bid of 100 at 99, when a second buy limit order of 50 at 99 is submitted, then price level 99 holds two orders totalling 150, and the newer order is at queue position 2.
  • Given two orders resting at 99, when the depth ladder is read, then level 99 reports total resting volume 150 and a count of 2 resting orders.
REQ-2

Crossing limit orders execute against the queue in order

A limit order priced at or through the opposite side executes against resting orders in queue order, taking each in turn until it is filled or the price is exhausted. It may fill against several resting orders and produce several trades. Any unfilled remainder rests at its limit price, at the back of that queue.

Acceptance criteria

  • Given asks at 101 of 40 (queue position 1) and 60 (queue position 2), when a buy limit order of 70 at 101 is submitted, then two trades are produced — 40 then 30, both at 101 — and the remaining ask at 101 has size 30.
  • Given a single ask of 40 at 101, when a buy limit order of 100 at 101 is submitted, then one trade of 40 at 101 is produced and the remainder of 60 rests as a bid at 101.
  • Given asks at 101 of 40 and at 102 of 50, when a buy limit order of 70 at 102 is submitted, then trades of 40 at 101 and 30 at 102 are produced in that order.
REQ-3

Market orders consume available liquidity

A market order executes against resting orders best-price-first, and within each price in queue order, until it is filled or the book is exhausted. It never rests.

Acceptance criteria

  • Given asks at 101 of 30 (position 1) and 20 (position 2), and at 102 of 50, when a buy market order of 80 is submitted, then trades of 30 at 101, 20 at 101 and 30 at 102 are produced in that order.
  • Given an empty ask side, when a buy market order of 10 is submitted, then no trade is produced and nothing rests on the book.
  • Given total resting ask volume of 50, when a buy market order of 80 is submitted, then 50 trades away, the ask side is empty, and the unfilled 30 is discarded rather than rested.
REQ-4

Individual orders can be cancelled

A cancellation removes one identified order. Where other orders rest at the same price, the level survives with its volume reduced, and the orders behind the cancelled one move up the queue while keeping their order relative to each other. A price level disappears only when its last order is removed. Cancelling an unknown or already-filled order is reported as unsuccessful rather than raising an error.

Acceptance criteria

  • Given bids at 99 of 100 (position 1), 50 (position 2) and 25 (position 3), when the position 2 order is cancelled, then level 99 holds two orders totalling 125, and the order formerly at position 3 is now at position 2.
  • Given a single bid of 100 at 99, when that order is cancelled, then cancellation reports success and price level 99 is absent from the book.
  • Given bids at 99 of 100 and 50, when the position 1 order is cancelled, then the remaining order is at position 1 and is next to fill.
  • Given an empty book, when an unknown order id is cancelled, then cancellation reports failure and the book is unchanged.
REQ-5

Synthetic order flow is reproducible

Order flow is produced by a seeded generator. The same seed must produce an identical sequence of events, so that a failing test can be reproduced exactly.

Acceptance criteria

  • Given two generators created with the same seed, when each produces 1000 events, then the two sequences are identical.
  • Given two generators created with different seeds, when each produces 1000 events, then the two sequences differ.
REQ-6

Order flow is a plausible mix

The generator emits new limit orders, cancellations of orders currently resting, and market orders, clustered around the current touch so that the book stays populated and active. Price levels routinely hold more than one order, so that queue behaviour is visible rather than theoretical.

Acceptance criteria

  • Given a generator run for 5000 events against a book, when the event types are counted, then all three of limit, cancel and market are present.
  • Given a generator run for 5000 events, when the book is inspected at each step, then both sides are non-empty for at least 95% of steps.
  • Given a generator run for 5000 events, when the best bid and best ask are inspected at each step, then at least one of them holds two or more resting orders for at least half of those steps.
REQ-7

Depth ladder

A vertical ladder of price levels, asks above and bids below, with the touch at the centre. Each level shows its price, its total resting volume, and the count of resting orders at that price, with volume drawn as a proportional horizontal bar. The ladder updates continuously as flow arrives.

Acceptance criteria

  • Given a book with five levels a side, when depth is requested for three levels, then exactly three bid levels and three ask levels are returned, best-first.
  • Given a level holding three orders totalling 220, when depth is returned, then that level reports volume 220 and an order count of 3.
  • Given fewer populated levels than requested, when depth is returned, then only the populated levels are returned rather than empty placeholders.
REQ-8

Queue view at the touch

The individual orders resting at the best bid and the best ask are shown separately, in queue order, each as a distinct element sized by its volume and labelled with its queue position. This is the Level 3 view: it makes visible what an aggregated ladder cannot — that a price level is a queue of separate orders, and that being early in it matters.

Acceptance criteria

  • Given a best bid holding three orders, when the queue at that price is read, then three orders are returned in arrival order, position 1 first.
  • Given the order at position 1 of the best ask is fully filled, when the queue is read again, then the order formerly at position 2 is now at position 1.
  • Given a side of the book is empty, when the queue for that side is read, then an empty queue is returned rather than an error.
REQ-9

Trade tape

Executed trades print in a running list, most recent first, each showing price, size and time. The list is bounded so it does not grow without limit.

Acceptance criteria

  • Given a tape bounded to 50 entries, when 60 trades occur, then the tape holds 50 entries and the oldest 10 have been discarded.
  • Given a single incoming order that fills against three resting orders, when the tape is read, then three separate trades are shown, not one combined entry.
  • Given a trade occurs, when the tape is read, then the newest trade is first.
REQ-10

Top-of-book readout

Best bid, best ask, mid price and absolute spread are displayed numerically and update as the book changes.

Acceptance criteria

  • Given a best bid of 99 and a best ask of 101, when the readout is computed, then mid is 100 and spread is 2.
  • Given a book with one side empty, when the readout is computed, then mid and spread are reported as unavailable rather than as a number.
REQ-11

Price chart

A rolling line chart plots the mid price over a fixed recent window, so that the effect of order flow on price is visible over time.

Acceptance criteria

  • Given a series bounded to 300 points, when 400 mid prices are recorded, then the series holds the most recent 300.
  • Given a period during which mid is unavailable, when the series is recorded, then those periods are omitted rather than recorded as zero.
REQ-12

Playback controls

The simulation can be paused and resumed. While paused the display holds its last state and no further flow is generated.

Acceptance criteria

  • Given a running simulation, when pause is activated, then no further events are applied to the book.
  • Given a paused simulation, when resume is activated, then events continue from the point at which it paused.
REQ-13

Legible without explanation

The curious observer of §2 — no market-structure background, no documentation — can tell unaided which side is buying, which is selling, and where the current price is. Sides are visually distinguished, the touch is the visual centre, and the queue view is labelled in words rather than jargon.

Acceptance criteria

  • Given the page at rest, when it is viewed, then bid and ask sides are distinguished by colour and are labelled in words.
  • Given the page at rest, when it is viewed, then the current mid price is the most prominent number on screen.
  • Given the queue view, when it is viewed, then it carries a plain-language label explaining that these are individual orders waiting their turn.

05Invariants

The requirements above describe what the application does in specific cases. The invariants below must hold for every input, and are verified by running randomised order flow through the engine and asserting each property after every event.

These are the correctness contract. Where an acceptance criterion checks one example, an invariant checks the entire input space — which is what makes it capable of catching a defect nobody thought to write a test for.

When one fails, the failing seed is printed. Re-running with that seed reproduces the failure exactly, per REQ-5.

INV-1

The book never crosses

Whenever both sides are populated, the best bid is strictly less than the best ask. A crossed book means two orders that should have matched did not.

INV-2

Volume is conserved

Matching neither creates nor destroys quantity. For any event, the total size traded equals the reduction in resting size on the passive side, and no order fills for more than its original size.

INV-3

Price–time priority is respected

Among orders resting at the same price, the one that arrived earliest fills first. No order may fill while an older order at the same price on the same side is still resting unfilled. This is a business rule rather than a structural property, and it is the one a naive implementation is most likely to get wrong.

INV-4

No trade-through

No execution occurs at a price worse than the best available on the passive side at the moment of execution. Liquidity is always consumed best-price-first.

INV-5

The book is well-formed

No resting order has a size of zero or less; no price level exists with no orders on it; and no cancelled or fully-filled order remains reachable.

INV-6

Queue integrity

At every price level, queue positions run contiguously from 1 with no gaps and no duplicates, and reflect arrival order. Removing an order — by fill or cancellation — closes the gap behind it without disturbing the relative order of those remaining. No order appears at more than one price level, or twice at the same one.

06Module contracts

The following boundaries are fixed by this document rather than chosen during implementation, so that each ticket extends a known structure instead of inventing one. A ticket that would require changing these signatures should say so and be re-specified.

ModuleResponsibilityMay import
public/src/engine.jsOrder book state and matching. Pure logic, no timers, no DOM.nothing
public/src/sim.jsSeeded synthetic order flow. No DOM.engine.js
public/src/render.jsDrawing to canvas and DOM. Holds no simulation state.nothing
public/src/app.jsWiring: the animation loop, controls, and glue.all of the above

Engine surface

// Side  is 'bid' | 'ask'.
// Order is { id, side, price, size, ts } — price omitted for market orders.
// Level is { price, volume, orderCount } — the aggregated view used by the ladder.
// Trade is { price, size, ts, aggressorSide, makerOrderId, takerOrderId }.
// Functions mutate the book and return their result.

export function createBook()                        // -> Book
export function addLimitOrder(book, order)           // -> Trade[]
export function submitMarketOrder(book, order)       // -> Trade[]
export function cancelOrder(book, orderId)           // -> boolean
export function bestBid(book)                        // -> Level | null
export function bestAsk(book)                        // -> Level | null
export function depth(book, maxLevels)               // -> { bids: Level[], asks: Level[] }
export function queueAt(book, side, price)           // -> Order[]  (arrival order, position 1 first)

queueAt is what makes this a Level 3 engine rather than a Level 2 one: it returns the individual resting orders, in queue order, behind the aggregate that depth reports. REQ-8 and INV-3 both depend on it.

Simulator surface

export function createSim(seed, config)              // -> Sim
export function step(sim, book)                      // -> Trade[]  (applies one event)

Rendering functions take a target and a plain data structure, and return nothing. Their exact signatures are settled by the ticket that first introduces each one.

07Constraints

NFR-1

Synthetic data only

All values are generated in the browser. No real, recorded, licensed or derived market data may enter this repository, and no runtime network call may be made. Any ticket requesting a real data source must be declined.

NFR-2

No dependencies, no build step

Native ES modules served as static files. Tests run under Node's built-in test runner. There is no package manager, no bundler and no lockfile — every removed moving part is one fewer way a live demonstration can fail.

NFR-3

Readable on a projector

Legible at the back of a meeting room: generous type, high contrast, and no information conveyed by colour alone.

NFR-4

Smooth at 60fps

Rendering keeps up with the simulation on a laptop without a discrete GPU. Drawing is decoupled from event generation so that a slow frame delays display, never the book.

NFR-5

Tests derive from the specification

Tests are written from this document and the ticket's acceptance criteria, and are committed before the implementation they cover. They are never written by reading the implementation back — that would only confirm the code does what it already does.

08Deliberately out of scope

The following are all reasonable, visible improvements that version 1.0 does not include. They are omitted on purpose: they are the surface on which further tickets are demonstrated, including tickets written by people who have never seen this document.

Order modification

Real venues let a resting order be amended rather than cancelled and replaced, and the priority rules are subtle: increasing size or changing price generally sends the order to the back of the queue, while reducing size generally retains its position. Version 1.0 supports only new orders and cancellations.

This is deliberately held back as a good future ticket. It is the clearest example in this document of a change where the outcome depends entirely on how precisely the rules are specified — a vague version produces something plausible and wrong, and a precise one produces something correct.

Everything else

  • Spread expressed in basis points
  • A volume-weighted average price line on the chart
  • Playback speed control
  • Colouring the tape by aggressor side
  • A cumulative depth curve
  • Highlighting the touch when it moves
  • The queue view at price levels beyond the touch
  • Showing how long each order has been resting
  • A light and dark theme
  • Session high and low
  • Top-of-book size imbalance
  • A control that injects a single large market order
  • Keyboard shortcuts

Nothing in this list should be built as part of version 1.0, even if it would be trivial to add while nearby.

09How this document is used

This specification is read by an AI agent on every ticket, and it is the reason the agent can accept some tickets and must decline others.

  1. A ticket cites requirements. "Implements REQ-8" links to a fixed definition rather than a shared assumption.
  2. Acceptance criteria become test names. The Given/When/Then above is written to read sensibly as a test description, because that is what it becomes.
  3. Tests are committed before implementation. Per NFR-5, so that a test cannot have been shaped by the code it checks.
  4. Invariants are checked continuously. Every change is run against §5, not only against the criteria for its own ticket.
  5. A ticket that cannot be tested is declined. If a ticket has no criterion that can be written as a test, the agent stops and asks for one rather than guessing.

Which is the whole argument in one line: what you specify is what you get. The quality of this document sets the ceiling on the quality of everything built from it.
